# ENSO spring-pump test — SUPPORTED on real data (= recharge-discharge oscillator)
Date 2026-05-30. Dylan's hypothesis: "in spring for ENSO there is a pump, and that pump
supplies the energy for the ENSO system." Channels: WWV anomaly (subsurface warm-water
battery), NINO3.4 SST (surface oscillation). All real (PMEL/NOAA, NOAA/PSL).

## Findings (seasonal climatology by calendar month)
1. NINO3.4 amplitude: LOUDEST Dec (0.99), QUIETEST Jun (0.56). Classic seasonal phase-locking.
2. Surface BUILD rate |dNINO/dt|: fastest in APRIL (boreal spring). The surface signal gets
   its biggest kicks in spring.
3. WWV battery recharge dWWV/dt: fastest DISCHARGE in spring (Mar most negative; Feb-Jun all
   negative). The subsurface battery drains into the surface in spring.
4. WWV LEADS NINO3.4 (positive lead-lag) -- recharge-oscillator signature.

## Reading
Spring is the energy hand-off: the deep warm-water battery DISCHARGES (Mar) and the surface
ENSO oscillation BUILDS fastest (Apr), then matures to its loud December peak. Dylan's "spring
pump" = the moment energy crosses from the subsurface reservoir into the visible oscillation.

## Matches established physics (web-confirmed)
- Recharge-discharge oscillator (Jin 1997): El Nino = WWV discharge; La Nina = recharge; WWV
  leads Nino3.4 and is an early predictor.
- Seasonal phase-locking: ENSO amplitude grows in boreal spring, peaks in December, declines
  the following March -- exactly our [1]+[2].
- Spring predictability barrier: low surface persistence in spring (the system is being
  re-seeded); WWV has NO spring barrier (the battery keeps its memory) -- why the subsurface
  pump is the right place to look.
