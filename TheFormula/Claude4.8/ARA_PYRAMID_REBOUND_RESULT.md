## The geometry, made literal

| pyramid element | ENSO variable | data |
|---|---|---|
| apex grain (measured) | surface temperature | NINO 3.4 SST anomaly |
| base grain — warm | western warm pool | WWV_west anomaly |
| base grain — cool | eastern volume | WWV_east anomaly |
| downward pressure + spring-back | apex↔base rate coupling | off-diagonal of the propagator M |

State `x = [T, warm, cool]`. One-step propagator `M` fit on the pre-2017 window.
Forecast at lead *h* is the rebound integrated forward, `x(t+h) = M^h · x(t)`.
Mathematically this is a Linear Inverse Model; the ARA contribution is the framing
and the specific prediction that the **three-body rebound restores amplitude**.

## The rebound is real and it has the right period

The fitted propagator's intrinsic dynamics:

| eigenmode | magnitude | period | damping half-life |
|---|---|---|---|
| **oscillation (the spring-back)** | \|λ\| = 0.966 | **38.1 mo ≈ 3.17 yr** | 20 mo |
| decay | λ = +0.79 | — | — |

The ~3.2-year oscillation **was not put in** — it emerges from the coupling between
the three grains. It sits squarely in ENSO's observed band and matches the
established LIM "ENSO mode." The spring-back Dylan described is a measurable,
correctly-tuned oscillation living in the three-body coupling.

## What the numbers say

1. **Amplitude is restored by the rebound, at every horizon** (e.g. 0.41 → 0.59 at
   12 mo, 0.53 → 0.67 at 6 mo). Integrating the coupled three-body dynamics swings
   harder than the damped static fit — exactly the predicted direction.

2. **At ~6 months the rebound wins outright**: bigger amplitude (0.67 vs 0.53) at
   essentially equal skill (0.352 vs 0.363). Inside the driver's lead horizon the
   third coordinate earns its keep for free.

3. **At 12–18 months the amplitude costs skill** (0.189 → 0.086 at 12 mo). Larger
   swings that are slightly mistimed score worse on squared error than a small hedge
   toward neutral. This is a genuine **amplitude-vs-error trade-off**, not a bug.

## The wall (honest limit)

The exact size and timing of each event is set partly by **stochastic wind forcing**
(westerly wind bursts) that has not happened at forecast time. A minimum-error
forecast therefore *correctly* damps — it refuses to commit to a peak whose final
size is unknowable. Forcing the amplitude back via the rebound gives realistic-sized
events but cannot time them exactly. So the amplitude still missing beyond the
rebound is **irreducible noise, not a missing coordinate**. No deterministic model —
three grains or thirty — closes it.

## Resolution

- **Three coordinates** generate the dynamics, the oscillation, and the amplitude.
  The pyramid is the correct *anatomy* and the rebound is a correct *mechanism*:
  the coupling oscillates at ENSO's period and restores the swing size.
- **Two coordinates** (apex + recharge sum) give the minimum-error *point forecast*.
- The leftover amplitude is **weather** — stochastic forcing the geometry does not
  reach.

This is the same classification-vs-forecast boundary the rest of the corpus keeps
landing on, now at the finest grain reached: the geometry describes and generates in
three; the deterministic point forecast optimises in two; the gap between them is noise.

## Negative results worth keeping (these did *not* earn their place)

- **Nonlinear product beat** (apex × driver): no holdout lift over the linear coupling.
- **Explicit warm/cool relation (tilt) as an added input**: identical holdout score
  to the recharge sum — the relation is real anatomy but forecast-redundant, because
  the eastern grain ≈ the NINO region (1-month lead) and the western signal is already
  inside the sum.

The relation and the nonlinear beat are real structure; they are not independent
*forecast* coordinates. The rebound (coupled rate dynamics) is the form of "three"
that does measurable work.
