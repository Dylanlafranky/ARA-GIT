The heart's forecast horizon & the independent third leg

Two-band ECG cross-system test · strict-causal · correlation-led · exploratory, small-n

1. Horizon ladder — how far ahead we can see depends on the slowest driver we measure

RR internal bands → a few beats Breath (Fantasia) → ~3 beats / seconds Oxygen (apnea) → ~1 minute

Each genuinely-slower real driver stretches the heart's forecast horizon to match its own timescale. The few-beats wall was never the heart's floor — it was just the slowest driver we had looked at. Direction is always slow-gates-fast (the slow band lends memory to the fast one); "fast accumulates into slow" has not appeared in any test.

2. Which third leg closes the lock? (slpdb, n=4, same subject: heart + breath + blood pressure + brain)

Lift in prediction correlation over heart-alone, averaged across 4 patients, at horizon = 5 beats (~4 s). Positive = the driver adds real forecast skill.

Blood pressure (the autonomic / baroreflex signal) is the leg that tightens the lock — it is genuinely independent of the breathing cycle. Raw brain (EEG band-power) and breath add little. The reading: by the time the nervous system's command reaches the heart it has already become a blood-pressure change, so the heart listens to the pressure, not the raw brain waves.

3. Full horizon sweep — lift over heart-alone (correlation)

Persistence baseline shown for reference. BP stays positive across the sweep; everything fades past ~20 beats in these non-apneic sleepers.

Honest scope. This is one cross-system line of evidence (n=4 sleepers, plus the earlier apnea and breath tests). Lifts are real but modest (+0.05 to +0.11 correlation) and these calm sleepers have no big slow oxygen swings, so the horizon does not extend dramatically here. The principle that landed: an independent upstream leg (blood pressure) helps, while the anti-phase mirror (CO₂) did not — exactly what the triangulation idea predicts. Needs confirmation on a dataset that has big oxygen swings and blood pressure on the same person.
