# The Coupling Axis, the Trophic Mirror, and the Rung-Drop (poop)

**Date:** 1 June 2026 · ARA framework (Dylan La Franchi & Claude)
**Status:** real test + honest record. Scored at the right scale (whole-wave turnover), the donor–consumer
mirror holds; the parts that "re-describe known ecology" are flagged, and the framework-specific parts
(reach-scaling, peer-separation, local mirror) are called out.

---

## Two orthogonal relationships on the φ-line

A system's place on the φ-line (ARA, 0 space ↔ 2 time) is *what it is*. There are then **two different
kinds of relationship**:

- **ALONG the φ-line** — dealings with neighbours at similar ARA: predation between peers, competition,
  step-to-step trophic transfer. Lion→zebra lives here (both mid-line consumers).
- **ACROSS the φ-line** — the **coupling / fuel** relationship: your energy source sits toward the
  *opposite* pole. Anchored to **energy origin**, which always bottoms out at the time pole (light →
  autotrophs). Plant↔light and whale↔krill live here.

These are independent: *what you are* (position) vs *what fuels you* (coupling).

## The donor–consumer mirror — tested, scale-correct

**Claim:** an energy source sits more toward *time* than its consumer, and the more *space*-extreme the
consumer, the *faster* (more-time) the source it must reach — to supply the space↔time conversion at the
rate opposite its own.

**The scale that matters (Dylan's correction):** score the food by its **own whole-wave turnover**
(regrowth / generation cycle) *relative to the consumer's life cycle*, **not** by the food's body size.
A blade of grass is small (looks "space") but regrows in weeks — against an elephant's decades it is the
*fast / time* source. Ratio = consumer_lifecycle / food_turnover ( >1 = source cycles faster = time side ).

| consumer | life (yr) | food | turnover (yr) | ratio | read |
|---|--:|---|--:|--:|---|
| Oak tree | 300 | light | ~0 | ~10⁶ | source faster ✓ |
| Blue whale | 85 | krill | 0.30 | 283 | ✓ |
| Elephant | 65 | grass | 0.05 | 1300 | ✓ (herbivore!) |
| Cow | 20 | grass | 0.05 | 400 | ✓ |
| Hummingbird | 4 | nectar | 0.01 | 400 | ✓ |
| Anteater | 15 | ants | 0.20 | 75 | ✓ |
| Tuna | 15 | small fish | 1.0 | 15 | ✓ |
| Cat | 15 | mouse | 1.0 | 15 | ✓ |
| **Lion** | 15 | **zebra** | 12 | **1.2** | **PEER — along-line** |

**Result: 9/9 genuine source pairs have the source cycling faster (more-time) than the consumer** —
herbivores included once scored by turnover. The earlier "herbivore failure" was a scoring error
(plant *body* = space) not a real break.

**Two framework-specific signals (the parts that aren't just the trophic pyramid):**
1. **Reach scales with extremity:** corr(log consumer lifespan, log turnover-ratio) = **+0.44**. The more
   space/slow the consumer, the faster the source it reaches — oak→light (~10⁶), whale→krill (283),
   cat→mouse (15).
2. **Peer-predation separates itself:** lion→zebra ratio ≈ **1.2** — the zebra turns over almost as slowly
   as the lion, so it reads as along-line (a near-peer), not a coupling. The test flagged it automatically.

## The local (fractal) mirror

The mirror is **local, not global**. Each organism rotates around **its own midpoint** and reaches the
**nearest opposite in its own ecosystem**. The closer to the centre (1.0) it sits, the **shorter its 0→2
span**, so the shorter its reach. The only *full* pole-to-pole mirror is the autotroph↔light extreme
(0 ↔ 2); everyone else gets a **partial reach** to a courier (krill, mice), and that's correct — the span
itself is shorter in the middle. This is the framework's vertical/fractal ARA: the 0–2 structure repeats
at every ecosystem scale, each with its own midpoint to rotate around.

## Metabolism = the conversion across the line

The body is a **converter parked across the φ-line**. It imports time-energy (sugar, traced to light) and
*routes* it: spend as **time** (burn — move/think/heat) or bank as **space** (store as fat via
**de novo lipogenesis** — a literal carbohydrate→fat pathway, time→space). Digestion / cellular respiration
runs it the other way (space-structure → fast usable ATP = space→time). And the conversion happens a rung
**below** the organism — *we don't digest our food, our cells do* — the organism just sets the **volume**
demand (low-energy-density food → high throughput; why grazers eat constantly).

## The rung-drop: poop (Dylan, the fun one — and it's the real shed)

Every cycle, after the usable energy is taken, the **residue is expelled DOWN a rung**. For an animal that
residue is **poop** — the leftover of the eat→convert→excrete cycle. This is the literal instance of the
framework's *shed / rent*: the part of the packet that isn't forwarded (the ~2−φ leftover, in the ideal
picture) is **handed down a rung**, where the **rung below recycles it** — egesta → decomposers / soil →
re-absorbed by plants (the producer rung). Poop is an animal's **leaf-fall**: the same "shed falls a rung
and is recycled" loop (leaf → soil → tree) one branch over. It closes the across-φ coupling into a cycle:
light → plant (space store) → animal (burns some, shed falls) → soil → plant again.

**Honest fences:** this is an *illustrative mapping*, not yet a measured law. A testable angle does fall
out of it, though: **digestive efficiency / egesta fraction should vary with trophic position** —
herbivores extract little and shed a lot (cellulose is hard; big poop), carnivores extract more and shed
less. So "shed fraction vs ARA position" is a real future test (does the shed sit near 2−φ ≈ 0.382 for any
class, or does it just track digestibility?). Ties to the pending decisive test "shed info falls a rung and
is recycled" and to `THE_BEDROCK_REFERENCE.md` (the 2−φ floor).
