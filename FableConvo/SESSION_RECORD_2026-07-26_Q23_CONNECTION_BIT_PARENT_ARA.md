# Session record — Q23 connection-heavy web versus logical bit

**Date:** 26 July 2026  
**Participants:** Dylan La Franchi and Codex/Sol

## Trigger

Dylan proposed that Q20-Q22 had mostly measured a time/information-heavy expression and that the next step was to
find its larger, connection-heavy counterpart, ARA that counterpart and the logical bit separately, then move one
rung up:

> “if it ends up at 1 we know they're coupled”

## Translation into a frozen test

The sixteen child-to-child handovers were treated as an Information³ relation web rather than matter. Its primary
connection identity was persistence of the complete web between two halves of each 250-shot block. The bit
identity was logical retention versus flip. Both were separately normalized to their own `0-2` diameters before
constructing:

\[
P=2B/(C+B).
\]

The real coupling criterion was frozen as correct-pair ridge closeness beyond shifted, wrong-bit, spatially broken
and permutation controls. This protected the test from a cheap aggregate `1.0`.

## Result

On a previously untouched distance-7 Willow patch, all four parent medians were near `1`, but the correct pair was
not closer than broken relations. Frozen verdict: **NOT SUPPORTED**, `3/10` gates. Independent validation passed
`117/117`.

The methodological refinement is:

> A parent reading near `1.0` can be a valid coarse-grained ridge, but it identifies a particular coupling only
> when the correct lower-level pairing approaches that ridge more strongly than relation-broken controls.

## Loose thread

The predeclared anti-child handover decomposition was weakly positive in all four datasets. Its post-result mean
rank relation was `0.07084`, pooled permutation `p=0.0226`. It is not promoted, but it is a precise candidate for
a future frozen single-lineage test using still-sealed distance-7 round counts.
