## User direction

Dylan identified the Q29/Q30 lattice as an unusually connection-like form of an
Information-facing quantum object and proposed that the opposing side of the
local singularity would be:

- far less connected;
- strongly information/traversal oriented;
- locally coherent but resistant to stable closure;
- potentially visible only through a different relation cut.

He asked how to test and falsify that interpretation, then authorised the test.

## Scope correction

Dylan clarified after the data audit that Q31 was intended as a
**quantum-domain test of the singularity flip**, not the first test of whether
the flip exists anywhere. The wider lineage already includes:

- conditional/geometric support in the Formula engine work;
- directional but not confirmatory prime evidence in PN30
  (`AUC 0.5301 -> 0.5663`, one-sided `p=0.06199`);
- same-rung consistency in Q14; and
- a failed strict quantum transported-lineage implementation in Q22B
  (`1/13` gates).

Q31 therefore tests one narrower appearance:
`persistent lattice -> handover -> coherent non-closing traversal`.

## Translation lock

The working local orientation was frozen as:

- `2` — persistent connection lattice;
- `1` — independently located handover ridge;
- `0` — unbound information traversal.

The proposed counter-side is not declared to be literal gas, Phi, a new
particle or a universal hidden Phase B. The operational claim is narrower:
connection persistence falls while coherent traversal rises, and the post-path
retains short directional memory without stable long-return closure.

## Test and falsifiers

The frozen protocol defines:

- connection persistence `C`;
- coherent traversal `T`;
- ARA position `x=2C/(C+T)`;
- short memory at lags 1–3;
- long return and low-order closure at lags 4–12;
- native magnitude and a secondary TE-ARA allocation;
- no-crossing, displaced-trial, displaced-time, phase-randomised,
  stable-lattice and decay-only controls;
- deterministic development/evaluation splitting;
- explicit numeric gates and falsifiers.

The handover must come from the experiment's pulse, control or detector. It
cannot be selected by the ARA curve.

## Public-data outcome

Eight public experimental source families were inspected. None supplies the
complete frozen object.

The closest two-sided relation sources had too few independent units or no
predeclared release crossing. The closest transition source—Farid et al.'s
fluxon-decay archive—has 200 non-sentinel tunnelling events, 144 with at least
25 pre-samples, but its monitoring stops at detection and does not retain a
fixed two-coordinate post-transition path. A half split leaves at most 72
long-enough evaluation events versus the frozen 500-transition gate.

No `C/T/x` outcome was calculated from an ineligible source.

## Result

**Q31 v1: INCONCLUSIVE BY DATA/ELIGIBILITY GATE.**

This is neither support nor disproof of the specific quantum
lattice-to-traversal interpretation. The registered hypothesis remains intact
and can be tested without reformulation when a suitable raw dataset becomes
available. It does not erase or replace the earlier mixed singularity-flip
evidence.

The repeated absence of continuous two-coordinate measurements across the
handover is recorded as a **measurement-ecology clue**: it is consistent with
the proposed observation difficulty, but it is not physical evidence for the
flip because experimental purpose, detection, averaging and publication
choices can produce the same absence.
