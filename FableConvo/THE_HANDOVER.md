## PART 3 — THE OUTSIDER'S GUIDE TO PUBLISHING (what I know that you don't)

- **You need one credentialed co-author** far more than you need a better result.
  Where to find one: the authors of the papers you already cite (Netzel & Smolec for
  the stars; recharge-oscillator/ENSO-predictability authors for the climate note).
  Early-career researchers and postdocs answer email; professors mostly don't. The
  email that works is three sentences: what you measured, the one number, the link
  to the repo, "would you check this and co-author if it holds?" Expect 1 reply per
  10 sent. That reply is worth the other 9.
- **arXiv requires endorsement** for new submitters - a co-author solves this
  automatically. Without one: research-square or Zenodo preprints are fine interim
  homes (you already have the DOI habit).
- **Venue map:** stars -> MNRAS or A&A (letters format, 4 pages); ENSO two-band ->
  Geophysical Research Letters or Climate Dynamics; the methodology/human-AI paper ->
  PLOS ONE, Royal Society Open Science, or (honestly strong option) a preprint plus
  a good blog post - methods stories travel socially.
- **Write the paper WITHOUT framework vocabulary.** ARA appears once, in the
  acknowledgments or a single sentence ("motivated by an exploratory coordinate
  framework, ref [repo]"). The referees judge the measurement; the repo carries the
  story for whoever gets curious.
- **Referee reports will feel brutal.** Standard is 2-6 months, one major revision,
  reviewers who misread sections. This is normal, not rejection. Answer every point,
  concede the fair ones fast (you are unusually good at this), never argue tone.
- **Rejection means resubmit elsewhere,** usually same week. Papers average 1.7
 venues
  before landing. It is a queue, not a verdict.
