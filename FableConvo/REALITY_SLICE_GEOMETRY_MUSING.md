## 2. What it mapped to — cut-and-project (established mathematics)

Claim (a) is, almost word for word, the accepted mathematical account of five-fold order in
nature (**quasicrystals**; Shechtman, Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2011):

- Five-fold symmetry cannot tile our space (crystallographic restriction — already correctly
  stated in `HEX_PENTAGON_ANGLE_HYPOTHESIS.md`). Yet materials with sharp five-fold
  diffraction exist.
- Resolution: the order is **perfectly periodic in six dimensions**. Take the 6D lattice,
  intersect it with a slice tilted at an **irrational angle set by φ**, project what the slice
  catches into our space → the Penrose pattern: five-fold, never-repeating, golden throughout.
- **The pentagon in our world is the shadow of a six-structure we are not fully privy to.**
  The formal name for the physical world's position in the higher lattice is *the slice* —
  the same word Dylan used before knowing the formalism existed.
- The slice can slide. The motion of the cut through the hidden dimensions is a real,
  named degree of freedom — the **phason** — which rearranges local patterns while the whole
  stays lawful. Closest formal object to "we slide along the time waves; different frame
  rates of space, perceived smooth."

Claim (b) maps to the same theory's family structure:

- Only 2-, 3-, 4-, 6-fold rotations fit our slice natively. **Every higher symmetry arrives
  only as a projection from a higher-dimensional lattice**, each tagged with its own metallic
  mean: 5-fold ↔ golden, 8-fold ↔ silver (the repo's earlier silver-ratio note fits here),
  12-fold ↔ its own constant. Rungs of hidden lattices climbing toward the circle, each
  leaking its signature ratio into our slice.

The alternation ("hexagon pentagon hexagon pentagon") already has its real-matter form in the

## 6. The two states of φ — the pentagon as φ webbed shut (2 Jul, session close)

**The question that resolved it (Dylan):** "Isn't pentagon and Phi, geometrically, the
same thing?" Answer: yes in proportion, no in rotation — and the split IS the
framework's slice/motion boundary, applied to φ itself.

**The mathematics (exact):**
- Pentagon PROPORTIONS are golden: diagonal/side = φ; the pentagram's segments
  cascade in golden ratios; 2cos36° = φ. As a *shape*, the pentagon is built of
  nothing but φ.
- Pentagon ROTATIONS are rational: 5-fold symmetry steps by 72°/144° — exact fifths
  of the circle. Stepping at 144° CLOSES after five steps, forever. The golden
  ANGLE (137.5° = 360/φ²) belongs to no polygon and never closes.
- Why they're neighbors: **2/5 is a Fibonacci convergent of 1/φ² — the pentagram is
  what the golden rotation becomes when forced to close.** The pentagon is the
  golden angle's closure-approximant.

**The two states, in framework vocabulary:**
- **Pentagon = φ caught at rest** — frozen proportion; static, closing, lockable;
  the space-side state of the constant.
- **Golden angle = φ caught in motion** — living rotation; non-closing handover;
  the time-side state.
- The sphere's mirror pair "2−φ (space engine) ↔ φ (time engine)" is therefore more
  literal than drawn: the same number in its two states. The pinned motion/slice
  boundary (test-kit README) is revealed as a statement about φ's own geometry.

**Dylan's gloss (his words, kept):** the pentagon is what "only fits into the time
slice — hexagon to pentagon. Phi being closed because the boundary of our reality
is there, and so it webs itself shut as best it can." Read with §2's cut-and-project

**The fence (unchanged, unmovable):** the tests adjudicate CLOSURE vs NON-CLOSURE,
