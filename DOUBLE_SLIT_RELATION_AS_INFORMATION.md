# The double slit — wave-as-circle and 1+1=3, forced not imposed

**Date:** 1 July 2026. **Companion to:** `ARA_AS_TRIANGULATION_IN_TIME.md`. **Source:** dialogue (Dylan + Claude).

This is the physical showcase where the framework's core pictures appear **literally**, not as analogy — because in quantum mechanics the wave-as-circle isn't something we impose, it's already the math, and the "third thing" is *forced* by the algebra rather than painted on.

> *"Good physical showcase of this is the double slit experiment."* — Dylan
> *"Not just the circle, but the information 1+1=3 showing relations are additive based on its coupling and interactions."* — Dylan

---

## 1. The circle is literal

The quantum amplitude is ψ = A·e^(iθ) — a point on a circle, a phasor. It rotates as e^(−iEt/ℏ): the phase advances at a rate set by energy, **driven by time**. So the circle turns *because time turns it* — the quantum phase is time seen as rotation, exactly the claim that time is the current underneath every wave.

And only the **relative** phase is ever observable; the absolute phase of either path is invisible. Relations are readable, absolutes are not — the triangulation claim in its starkest physical form. Everywhere else we had to *map* things onto the circle; here the circle is standing in the foundational equation, undeniable.

## 2. 1 + 1 = 3 — the relation as information

With both slits open, the observable is **not** the sum of the two single-slit patterns:

**|ψ₁ + ψ₂|² = |ψ₁|² + |ψ₂|² + 2·Re(ψ₁ψ₂\*)**

- The first two terms, |ψ₁|² and |ψ₂|², are the slits taken **alone** — 1 and 1.
- The third term, **2·Re(ψ₁ψ₂\*)**, is in *neither* slit. It is literally the inner product of the two phasors — the overlap of the two circles, the relation drawn as a number.

Two waves, and a third thing that exists only in their coupling. That is 1+1=3, and you can point at exactly where it lives in the math. The relation is a real, readable entity, not a manner of speaking.

## 3. Two knobs — phase and coupling (three regimes, three landmarks)

> *"It isn't that they are decoupled, it's that its the anti-phase. or the cancellation ridge. When the two waves meet, they cancel each other out. I guess it could be described that way."* — Dylan

The third term has **two** knobs, and they are not the same thing:

**2·√(I₁I₂)·|γ₁₂|·cos δ**

- **cos δ** — the *phase*: which way the two waves are trading at this point on the screen.
- **|γ₁₂|** — the *coherence*: how strongly they are coupled at all (0 to 1).

Turning them gives three distinct outcomes, mapping to three different landmarks. (An earlier draft of this doc wrongly folded the middle one into the last — Dylan's correction.)

| regime | third term | local result | landmark | sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **in-phase** (δ=0, coherent) | +2√(I₁I₂), full | up to 4·I — reinforced | engine / adding | 1+1=**3** |
| **anti-phase** (δ=π, coherent) | −2√(I₁I₂), full | → 0 — cancelled | **cancellation ridge** | 1+1=**3** |
| **decohered** (\|γ₁₂\|→0, which-path) | 0 | I₁+I₂ — flat | **pole** | 1+1=**2** |

**The dark fringe is the ridge — this is the correction.** The cancellation is *not* decoupling. The two waves are **fully coherent** there (|γ₁₂|=1) and meet exactly anti-phase (δ=π), so the third term is at **full strength but negative** — it nulls them. The relation is **maximally alive** and *producing* the null. That is still 1+1=3 (the third term is present and doing the work); it is the phase+anti-phase cancellation that *defines* the 1.0 ridge.

**The pole is the other thing — decoherence / which-path.** There the third term does not go negative, it **dissolves to zero** (|γ₁₂|→0), and you get the flat classical sum everywhere, no fringes. *This* is the only true 1+1=2 — the relation is **gone**, not cancelling.

So losing a *dark fringe* and losing *all* fringes are opposite events: the dark fringe is the relation at full strength nulling (ridge); the flat screen is the relation absent (pole). Conservation ties the coherent case together — the bright surplus and the dark deficit balance back to I₁+I₂ globally, but locally the intensity swings from 0 to 4·I, and **that swing is the information**. Only the decohered pole gives a flat 2 with nothing swinging — no information. The cancellation ridge is a **local null inside a fully-alive relation**, which is exactly why it is the ridge and not the pole.

## 4. The "3" is information, not quantity

The third term adds **no net probability**. Integrate it over the whole screen and it is zero — the bright and dark fringes are a *redistribution*, energy conserved. So the extra thing is not a third unit of *stuff* but a third unit of *information* — pattern, structure, where things land — created purely by the coupling.

That is precisely "relation IS information": coupling adds **pattern without adding quantity**. If it added quantity it would be a bookkeeping error; adding information is the real claim, and it is exactly what the cross term does.

## 5. Why it's a good showcase — and the honest limit

**|a+b|² ≠ |a|² + |b|²** is *forced* by the algebra. The third term is not something we chose or painted on; it falls out of squaring a sum of complex amplitudes. So:

- "wave = circle, driven by time, relation-not-absolute" is the **literal content of ψ**, not a metaphor.
- "1+1=3, additive by coupling" is a **faithful reading of a hard mathematical fact**, not decoration.

That is why the double slit tracks the framework so cleanly. **The honest limit:** it remains the framework's *language describing* standard quantum mechanics — the cross term, the Born rule, decoherence are all textbook. It is **not** the framework *deriving* or *predicting* QM, and it should not be cited as evidence that it does. But when what you're describing is *forced* rather than chosen, the description earns more than a relabeling would: it means the picture is tracking something real about how coupled relations carry information — the circle, and the third term — rather than dressing a known result in new words.
