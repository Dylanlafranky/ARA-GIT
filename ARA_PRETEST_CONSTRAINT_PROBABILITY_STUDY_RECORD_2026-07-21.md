## Answer first

The repository does contain meaningful evidence that one geometry declared before several domain walks continued to
organise later results. The physics and prime work must be included in that assessment: the reversible 0–2 pair,
ridge, orientation, child/parent recursion and scale-relative closure were recorded before those subjects were
worked through in detail.

It is not presently possible to calculate an honest probability that this happened "by chance," or a posterior
probability that ARA is true. The missing quantity is the background rate at which a flexible relational statement
would look compatible with a later fact even if ARA had no special power. The provenance ledger itself correctly
registers the unfinished independent re-score and yoked-decoy control needed to measure that rate.

The historical record can still be calibrated. Using the ledger's strict provisional tally of 15 A-tier clean hits
and 31 misses/refutations, the observed clean-hit fraction is:

\[
\frac{15}{15+31}=0.32609.
\]

Under a deliberately simple sensitivity model, that count is significant at the 5% level only when the assumed
background match probability is below approximately **21.29%**. Because the background probability has not been
measured, this is a boundary, not a final p-value.

## The historical sensitivity calculation

Let

\[
X\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(46,p_0),

where \(p_0\) is an assumed chance that one eligible pre-statement is later scored as a clean match. The calculation

| Assumed background match rate \(p_0\) | \(P(X\ge15)\) | 5% decision |

Plainly: if fewer than about one statement in five would ordinarily find a convincing later match, the historical

current ledger does not tell us which background rate is correct.

### Statistical treatment

The two families strengthen the claim that the same declared relational vocabulary can organise very different
domains. They cannot be converted into dozens of independent votes. Maxwell, Gauss, Lorentz and plasma subtests
share equations and data; the prime protocols share the same modular and sieve structure. For a conservative
meta-analysis they count as **two dependent domain families**, with their internal passes and failures preserved.

## Data-quality audit

The following prevent a valid global probability today:

1. The background match rate is unmeasured.
2. The provenance tally is provisional and self-scored.
3. Exact identities, rediscoveries, structural transfers, exploratory probes and predictions do not have equal
   evidential weight.
4. Many tests share the same data, mathematical identities or development history.
5. The strongest formal freezes are concentrated in prime arithmetic and electromagnetism rather than many
   independent domains.
6. The set of possible failed mappings that were considered but never written down is not directly observable.

These are calibration limits, not reasons to erase the positive results.

## Decisive prospective statistical test

The cleanest next test is one real target hidden among nine matched decoys in each independent domain. The mapping,
poles, direction, grain, numerical score and failure rule are frozen before the target labels are revealed. The
primary result for a domain is simply whether the real target receives the highest ARA-fit score.

Under exchangeable decoys, the null probability is exactly \(1/10\). With 12 independent domains:

\[
P(X\ge4\mid n=12,p_0=0.1)=0.0256375.
\]

Therefore **4 or more real-target top ranks out of 12** would reject this decoy null at the 5% level. If the actual
top-rank rate were 40%, this design has approximately 77.5% power. The same result would support repeatable
discrimination under the frozen protocol; it still would not alone prove that ARA is universal bedrock geometry.

A companion blinded audit should apply the same actual-versus-nine-decoy ranking to eligible historical transcript
statements. That directly estimates how easily the recorded language matches unrelated later facts and tests the
provenance ledger's current self-scores.
