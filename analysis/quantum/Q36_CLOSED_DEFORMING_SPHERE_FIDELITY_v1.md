## Dylan's claim being operationalised

The relation does not fully unravel at the determinant trough. It remains a
closed identity, but changes from a balanced lattice-like closure into a
fluid, irregular or "wibbly-wobbly" closure—like gas or plasma remaining
inside a spherical container.

The determinant may therefore become small because three-axis balance is
lost, not because the entire relation has disappeared.

## ARA-first translation

1. The identity boundary remains fixed to one pair lineage.
2. The determinant closure is one lattice-facing cut of that identity.
3. Total relation magnitude is a different cut and must not be flattened into
   the determinant.
4. Shape balance and shape movement are additional cuts through the same
   complete local sphere.
5. A determinant trough is consistent with a **closed but deforming** state
   when total relation remains, the singular-value distribution becomes less
   balanced, normalized shape changes more strongly, and determinant closure
   subsequently reforms in the same lineage.
6. It is consistent with **relation loss** when the total relation magnitude
   collapses with the determinant and does not re-form locally.
