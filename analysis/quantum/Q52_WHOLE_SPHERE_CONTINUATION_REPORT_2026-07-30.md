## Plain-language result

Q50 and Q51 had established one outward directional reversal:

\[
0\longrightarrow2.
\]

Q52 extended the complete simulated state for another 1,500 interactions to
ask whether the **whole-sphere external direction** then:

1. actively returned, \(0\rightarrow2\rightarrow0\);
2. stopped near \(2\); or
3. depended on the future coupling path.

Neither universal answer passed.

The fixed coupling paths produced apparent geometric returns, but by the time
the coordinate returned their external movement had fallen to effectively
zero. An orientation calculated from an almost stationary vector is not an
active traversal, so the frozen movement guard correctly rejected it.

The alternating and random coupling paths remained active. At the individual
source-seed level, roughly two thirds of these mixed futures completed active
returns. When those differently directed children were combined, however,
their aggregate external direction sat close to the `1.0` ridge rather than
returning to `0`.

In ARA language, the most informative result is therefore:

\[
\underbrace{\text{active child returns on opposite sides}}_{\text{retained
local asymmetry}}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{x_{\rm parent}\approx1}_{\text{coarse parent ridge}}.
\]

That is consistent with the previously stated ARA rule that a parent can read
`1.0` because its children cancel at that measurement grain. It was not the
registered universal-return endpoint, so it remains a new diagnostic pattern
rather than a passed Q52 claim.

## Why the fixed-family “return” was rejected

For fixed A and fixed B, the circle-centre coordinate did eventually re-enter
the `x <= 0.5` region. The movement in those return bins was only about:

```text
fixed A: 1.60e-8 of historical movement
fixed B: 7.08e-9 of historical movement
```

This is a geometrical direction attached to an effectively extinguished
external movement. The protocol required at least `0.25` of the historical
movement baseline, so neither was counted as an active return.

The centroid sensitivity estimator independently placed both fixed futures
near `x = 1.999` with very low movement, while the extrema estimator ended
near the ridge. This estimator disagreement is another reason not to promote
the fixed-path result into a completed traversal.

## The child/parent split exposed by Q52

The six alternating/random families had active source-seed return fractions
between `63.6%` and `67.7%`. Yet their family-level circle coordinates ended
between approximately:

```text
0.899 and 1.076
```

Their continued movement remained strong. Thus the parent did not read `1.0`
because all children had become still. It read near `1.0` while differently
directed child trajectories remained active.

This provides a concrete simulator example of the distinction:

\[
\text{ridge by cancellation}
\neq
\text{ridge by resonance death}.
\]

That distinction is descriptive and post-result in Q52. A new frozen test
would need to predeclare the expected child-side balance and predict the
parent ridge from it before opening another continuation archive or simulator
family.

## Scientific interpretation

Q52 does **not** support the simple universal statement that this measured
whole-sphere coordinate must always complete an active
`0 -> 2 -> 0` cycle within the tested continuation.

It also rejects a simple universal one-way settling description for the
allowed futures.

The data instead distinguish three behaviours:

1. fixed coupling can extinguish external movement;
2. mixed coupling preserves active child returns;
3. coarse-graining those mixed children can place the parent near the
   directional ridge.

This makes the future coupling environment important to the observed
trajectory, but the frozen driver-dependence gate was not satisfied and
should not be relabelled after the result.

## Boundaries

- a universal physical ARA cycle;
