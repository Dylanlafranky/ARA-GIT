T376 — TE-ARA coupling and confound audit

Evidence class: post-hoc diagnostic. The prospective T376 verdict remains unchanged.

A=2q1/Q, B=2q2/Q, A+B=2. Empirical coupling strength is C=AB: 1 is equal two-end participation; 0 is one-end domination.

Answer first

The incoming event is strongly two-ended: median C=0.982. But this mainly describes how the solid scintillator and its two sensors shared the flash. Coupling had only ρ=+0.0213 association with later daughter time, and gain correction left it at ρ=+0.0090.

The strongest distortion is instrumental: 97.8% of held-out pulse pairs lie on the detector's discrete 1.25 ns grid. Run-dependent end imbalance also moves the apparent ARA position. These effects explain much of the ribs and skew, but they do not reveal a hidden individual decay clock.

Variables capable of skewing T376

  1. End-to-end gain and geometry: moves the apparent ridge away from 1.00; corrected separately within each run above.
  2. Total pulse Q: mixes deposited energy, track length and detector response. It was the best of the tested prediction models, but only slightly.
  3. Two-ended trigger requirement: removes events where one end is too weak, artificially filling the middle and emptying the poles.
  4. 1.25 ns digitisation: creates rational vertical ribs in x_mu.
  5. Run identity: calibration and geometry drift across days.
  6. Visible-daughter conditioning: this table excludes initial muons without a qualified visible daughter.
  7. 0.3–20 microsecond search window: truncates the observed lifetime distribution.
  8. Lower-counter veto and stopping geometry: selects a restricted subset of muon paths.
