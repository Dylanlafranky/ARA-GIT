# Muon test rundown: what was split, what led up, and what remains unseen

Date: 18 August 2026

## Answer first

The muon work did recover both a **split** and a **lead-up**, but the evidence exists at several different grains and must not be flattened.

1. **Population lead-up:** strongly observed/recovered. Across many stopped-pion and stopped-muon cycles, the fitted sequence is prompt neutrino crest, prompt/delayed branch equality, cumulative child-half, then the delayed muon-decay-neutrino crest.
2. **Decay-product split:** supported as an established-physics ARA crosswalk. The charged daughter is opposed by a joint two-neutrino branch; that neutral branch contains two asymmetric children. Most of the detailed two-neutrino work uses flavor templates or Standard-Model truth distributions rather than simultaneous experimental detection of both neutrinos.
3. **Pre-decay organisation:** population spin anti-phase and charged-daughter orientation are strongly recovered. A universal spin-maturity or 7.5-turn release clock is not.
4. **Individual advance warning:** not established. Static incoming detector relations generally failed to predict the later charged-daughter time beyond ordinary detector geometry. T408 is directionally positive after parent conditioning and child decompression, but its primary frozen confirmation rule still fails.

Therefore the careful statement is not simply “nothing happens before decay.” A structured population approach to the handover is visible, and several parent/child relations constrain the decay-product geometry. What is still missing is a single event record that watches one living muon's relevant pre-decay state and directly joins it to its charged daughter and neutral children.

## The physical timing statement

In the ordinary decay description,

\[
\mu^+\rightarrow e^+ + \nu_e + \bar\nu_\mu,
\]

the three daughters belong to one decay vertex. None of the ARA tests demonstrated that the two neutrinos are emitted at two separately resolved times.

The recovered “lead-up” instead refers to one of three things:

- the evolving population mixture before and through the delayed-release maximum;
- pre-decay population spin/phase organisation;
- an incoming detector relation that changes the probability of a later daughter-time window.

Those are meaningful precursors or relational stages, but they are not yet a frame-by-frame recording of two neutrino births from one muon.

## What “the dots” mean

In T408/T409, a dot is an event-linked incoming stopped-muon candidate paired with a later charged-daughter candidate. For a genuine muon decay, that event implies the decay produced the charged daughter and two neutrinos. But the plotted coordinates contain the incoming detector relation and charged-daughter delay; neither neutrino was measured.

The dots can therefore reveal **which incoming states are associated with later decay windows**. They cannot, by themselves, show the neutrino waveform, separate the two neutrino children, or mark an exact neutrino-birth coordinate.

## Current best conclusion

The tests support a multi-stage ARA account at population and relational levels:

\[
\text{parent population approach}
\rightarrow
\text{branch equality / child handover}
\rightarrow
\text{charged-versus-neutral split}
\rightarrow
\text{two asymmetric neutral children}.
\]

The missing rung is **direct individual linkage**. To test whether the lead-up is a genuine single-muon precursor rather than population organisation, one dataset must contain, under one event key:

1. repeated pre-decay muon spin/polarisation or another dynamic state;
2. charged-daughter energy and direction;
3. neutral-sensitive timing or reconstructed missing momentum;
4. the decay time, held out until prediction.

Until that exists, “lead-up to the population handover” is supported; “we watched one muon spawn its two neutrinos in stages” is not.
