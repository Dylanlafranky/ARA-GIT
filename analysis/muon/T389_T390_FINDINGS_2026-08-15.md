# T389–T390 findings — spin anti-phase and 7.5-turn release

Date: 2026-08-15

## Answer first

The earlier Phase-A candidate was the **directional population spin imprint** carried by the changing 96-detector daughter-count shares. It was not detector-voltage radius and not a directly observed individual muon trajectory. T382 compressed that imprint to the folded scalar

\[
x_C(t)=1-\cos\theta(t).
\]

T389 restored the perpendicular signed quadrature and tested the geometric Di-ARA

\[
z(t)=c(t)+i s(t),
\qquad
(x_c,x_s)=(1-c,1-s).
\]

The physical spin anti-phase is the joint half-turn inversion

\[
z(t+T/2)=-z(t),
\qquad
(x_c,x_s)\mapsto(2-x_c,2-x_s).
\]

This two-axis anti-phase was supported in all three untouched 63 G, 160 G and 400 G holdouts. The separate claim that population decay/neutrino production is concentrated at exactly 7.5 accumulated turns was not supported.

## T390 — exactly 7.5 turns and population release

The 96 detectors were summed so that directional spin acceptance would cancel as far as the instrument permits. A frozen exponential parent was removed, then equal `+/- 1/8`-turn release windows were scored at each field's own 7.5-turn time:

| Field | 7.5-turn time | Observed/expected | Mean Pearson residual |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 63 G | 8.625833 microseconds | 0.981570 | -1.575587 |
| 160 G | 3.396422 microseconds | 1.000853 | +0.235633 |
| 400 G | 1.358569 microseconds | 1.000517 | +0.231253 |

The pooled ratio was `0.99868785`, a deficit of `0.131215%`. It ranked 7th among the eight eligible half-integer landmarks, did not exceed the control 97.5th percentile `1.00180292`, and had detector-bootstrap excess interval `[-0.00356218, +0.00106866]`. All five frozen gates failed.

Therefore this dataset does **not** support “7.5 spins triggers the neutrino-producing decay.” Muon decays still produce the charged daughter and two neutrinos; the result says their population timing is not detectably locked to this particular accumulated-turn landmark.

## T391 follow-up — raw detector-field anti-phase

T391 removed T389's learned cosine/sine spatial decoder and retained only the
cadence learned on the 20/25 G calibration runs. The complete 96-detector
share residual was phase-folded into 48 bins, then tested directly for
`y(theta+pi) = -y(theta)`.

All six frozen gates passed. Raw half-turn correlations were `-0.75114`,
`-0.70052` and `-0.25331` at 63, 160 and 400 G, with their most negative
locations at `0.49`, `0.51` and `0.51` turns. Full inversion beat direct and
both one-bank inversions in every field. Correct detector labels beat all 95
cyclic shifts, and correct cadence beat both wrong-field cadences. The pooled
advantage was `0.50142`, with hierarchical-bootstrap 95% interval
`[0.19135,0.68616]`.

The anti-phase therefore exists in the raw population detector field before
two-axis compression. This strengthens the geometric identification without
changing the release boundary: it still does not observe or predict an
individual muon decay or neutrino.

## Combined interpretation

The two findings separate geometry from release law:

1. The measured population spin relation has the expected two-axis Phase-A/anti-phase structure and is strongest at a half turn.
2. Completing 7.5 such turns is not a supported release trigger.
3. Recovering a geometric anti-phase is therefore insufficient to predict the later neutrino-producing handover.
4. The remaining decisive target is still a separately measured **pre-decay retained-connection child** linked to an individual or narrowly grouped decay outcome.
