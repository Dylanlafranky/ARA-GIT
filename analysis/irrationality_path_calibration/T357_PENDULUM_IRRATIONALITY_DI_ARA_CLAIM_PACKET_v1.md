## Claim under test

The T348 path/history instrument can distinguish a physically repeating pendulum path from a coupled child path that remains coherent while failing to close over the same parent-cycle horizon.

This is a test for **structured physical non-closure**, not a proof that any finite measured frequency ratio is mathematically irrational.

## Frozen ARA translation

- One pendulum sampled by its own cycle is the finite/reused, relation-determined closure reference.
- In a double pendulum, arm 1 supplies the parent clock and arm 2 supplies the child path.
- Continued opening of child addresses is measured by `x_P`.
- Movement left unexplained by ordered history is measured by `x_R`.
- Exact closure, coherent miss and incoherent wandering are retained separately in `C(H)`.
- Pure endpoints are reference poles. Physical identities may occupy gradients.

## Confirmation pattern

The physical structured-nonclosure claim is supported only if:

1. the single-pendulum reference remains on the finite/reused and relation-determined side and closes at one parent cycle;
2. the double-pendulum child opens more relational addresses than the single reference;
3. the double path retains ordered-history information, rather than becoming equivalent to a shuffled path;
4. the double path shows coherent nonzero cycle miss more often than exact one-cycle closure;
5. mismatching parent and child lineages weakens prediction or coherent closure;
6. reversal preserves support and unsigned closure structure while reversing traversal orientation.

## Falsifiers

- The single reference does not recover one-cycle closure.
- The double child does not open more addresses than the single reference.
- Shuffling chronology does not reduce history information.
- The double path is indistinguishable from stochastic wandering (`x_R` high and closure coherence low).
- Broken lineage performs as well as the true parent-child pairing.
- Results depend on one record only and do not replicate across at least two of the three declared run types.
