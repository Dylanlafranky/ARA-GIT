# Frozen protocol — Phi as a four-child handover

**Frozen:** 30 July 2026, before numerical inspection of
`DoubleDataWithControl_2_Dt_0_0001.mat`.

**Status:** ARA-first prospective test on a previously unopened endpoint from
the public dynamicslab *MultiArm-Pendulum* archive. The first driven
double-pendulum run is development; the second is the frozen evaluation.

## 1. Proposal

The preceding single-child-turn test located ordinary pole/opposition locking,
not Phi. Dylan then proposed that Phi may belong to the collective handover of
the four children produced by two coupled A/B parents:

\[
(A,B)\times(A,B)
\longrightarrow
\left(C_{AA},C_{AB},C_{BB},C_{BA}\right).
\]

The four children need not be equal. Their retained inequality may prevent
complete child cancellation while the parent remains a complete identity.

This protocol separates:

1. **Phi specificity:** does the four-child allocation have the particular
   non-repeating inequality generated by four successive Phi steps?
2. **asymmetry–maintenance:** does a more unequal four-child allocation predict
   better retention of the parent's following cycle?

Either claim may pass without the other.

## 2. Data and freeze

Public archive: dynamicslab *MultiArm-Pendulum*, Zenodo
`10.5281/zenodo.6633719`.

- development only: `DoubleDataWithControl_1_Dt_0_0001.mat`;
- frozen endpoint: `DoubleDataWithControl_2_Dt_0_0001.mat`.

Only raw timestamps, two arm angles and their recorded angular velocities are
used. No Fourier transform, Hilbert phase, SVD/POD, learned model or equation of
motion enters the endpoint.

## 3. Parent cycle and four children

Each angle is circular-mean centred. A parent cycle runs from one negative-to-
positive centre crossing of arm 1 to the next, with positive recorded arm-1
velocity. Crossings closer than `0.4 × 1.333 s` are merged using the previously
audited pendulum spacing.

At every raw time slice, the signs of the two centred arm angles define the
ordered four-child square:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{child}&(\operatorname{sgn}q_1,\operatorname{sgn}q_2)\\
\hline
C_{AA}&(+,+)\\
C_{AB}&(+,-)\\
C_{BB}&(-,-)\\
C_{BA}&(-,+)
\end{array}
\]

For each parent cycle, the child occupancy vector

\[
\mathbf p=(p_{AA},p_{AB},p_{BB},p_{BA}),\qquad \sum_i p_i=1
\]

is calculated directly from sample counts.

## 4. Phi quartet and controls

Let \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). Four successive non-closing Phi steps partition the
circle into the gap multiset

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf g_\phi=
\left(\phi^{-4},\phi^{-3},\phi^{-2},\phi^{-3}\right)
}
=
(0.145898,\ 0.236068,\ 0.381966,\ 0.236068).
\]

It sums to one and supplies a precise version of “one Space-like share near
`0.382`, distributed through four unequal children.” Because the pole
orientation and starting child are arbitrary, every circular rotation and
reflection is allowed, but arbitrary permutations are not.

The cycle's total-variation distance to the nearest allowed orientation of
each template is compared:

- **Phi quartet:** \(\mathbf g_\phi\);
- **equal quarters:** `(0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25)`;
- **paired/dyadic:** `(0.5, 0, 0.5, 0)`;
- **linear irregular:** normalized `(1,2,3,4)`.

Phi passes template specificity only if it has the smallest frozen-run median
distance.

## 5. Collective completion location

For every cycle that visits all four children, record the earliest fractional
cycle position \(u_4\in[0,1)\) at which the fourth distinct child is first
visited.

Its circular distance to the following predeclared landmarks is compared:

- Phi: `{0.381966, 0.618034}`;
- poles: `{0}`;
- ridge/opposition: `{0.5}`;
- quarters: `{0.25, 0.75}`;
- thirds: `{1/3, 2/3}`.

Phi passes completion specificity only if its frozen-run median distance is
the smallest.

## 6. Child inequality and parent maintenance

Four-child inequality is

\[
I_4=1-\frac{-\sum_i p_i\log p_i}{\log4}.
\]

`0` is equal occupancy; `1` is one-child dominance.

Parent amplitude retention from cycle \(n\) to \(n+1\) is

\[
R_P=
\frac{\min(A_n,A_{n+1})}{\max(A_n,A_{n+1})},
\qquad
A_n=\max(q_1)-\min(q_1).
\]

The primary association is Spearman correlation between \(I_4(n)\) and
\(R_P(n)\). A one-sided circular-shift test with `5,000` shifts and seed
`20260730` asks whether the observed positive association exceeds temporal
alignment expected from the same two series. Shifts are at least five complete
cycles.

The same frozen control is applied to Phi-template proximity
\(1-d_{\rm TV}(\mathbf p,\mathbf g_\phi)\) versus \(R_P\).

Period retention and next-cycle child-allocation retention are saved as
secondary diagnostics and cannot change the verdict.

## 7. One-wave quadrant diagnostic

The four sign quadrants of `(q1, dTheta1)` are also measured. This is a
diagnostic that distinguishes:

- four children created by the two coupled parents; from
- the ordinary four directional quadrants within one wave.

It cannot change the primary verdict.

## 8. Frozen verdict

Four equally weighted check families:

1. Phi quartet beats every allocation template;
2. four-child completion is closest to Phi;
3. child inequality positively predicts parent retention with shifted
   `p < 0.05`;
4. Phi-template proximity positively predicts parent retention with shifted
   `p < 0.05`.

- **SUPPORTED:** `4/4`;
- **MIXED:** `2–3/4`;
- **NOT SUPPORTED:** `0–1/4`.

## 9. Boundaries

- The fixed four-child labels are an ARA representation of the two raw angle
  signs. A result does not prove that these are ontologically fundamental
  children.
- Template fitting allows only rotations/reflections fixed before evaluation.
- TE-ARA closure is not evidence because the occupancy shares sum to one by
  construction.
- A positive inequality result would support asymmetric child maintenance, not
  Phi unless the two Phi-specific checks also pass.
- No rule will be changed after opening run 2.

## 10. Post-freeze clarification from Dylan

After the result was opened, Dylan clarified that the intended proposal was
not “maximum inequality produces maximum retention.” It was:

> The four children retain a non-zero, non-resonant asymmetry while remaining
> inside the complete TE-ARA budget. This maintained irregularity prevents
> terminal child closure and allows the parent cycle to continue.

The frozen monotonic \(I_4\rightarrow R_P\) check was therefore an over-strong
translation by Codex. Its negative result remains part of the frozen record,
but it does not falsify Dylan's clarified bounded-asymmetry claim. Testing that
claim requires child ordering, phase recurrence and closure—not occupancy
inequality alone.
