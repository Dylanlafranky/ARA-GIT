# Fresh test — does the ephemeral end fill the OPPOSITE pole?

**Date:** 1 Jul 2026. **Fresh test** (independent of any prior river-erosion work). **Script:** `ephemeral_pole_test.py` · **Figure:** `ephemeral_pole_test.png`
**Replicability:** every timescale is an order-of-magnitude value from a cited public source, listed in the script header; the script prints a provenance table and is deterministic (seed fixed). Anyone re-running reproduces the figure.

## Question
Earlier, the solid-body ladder clustered near ONE pole (persist ≫ form → near-inert). Was "both poles filled" real, or my rock-bias projecting? The clean check: add the high-turnover / ephemeral end (built slow or reworked fast) and see if it lands at the *opposite* pole from real data.

**Both poles are occupied by different populations** — this is the key point. The inert bodies (boulder, asteroid) jam against the **lock/frozen pole**; the soft surface features (soil, loess) cross the ridge to the **dissipation pole** — built slowly, erased fast, structure never handed forward. They are entirely different materials/processes, so the two-pole filling is *not* one biased population projected twice.

**The ridge is no longer arbitrary.** A *real* feature anchors it: the sand ripple, which forms and is remade on the same ~tens-of-minutes timescale (build = destroy, log-ratio 0.00). Pure turnover holding a statistical form while every grain cycles = the clock/maintenance reading of the 1.0 ridge.

## Framework reading (consistent with the two-deaths idea)
- **Lock pole** (boulder/asteroid) = "lock-shut" — frozen, no exchange, form held indefinitely.
- **Dissipation pole** (soil/loess) = "can't-hand-over" — built then erased faster than rebuilt, information not carried forward.
- **Ridge** (ripple) = the clock — form = destroy, identity maintained through continuous turnover.
- **Nothing sits *on* the φ-engines** (0.382 / 1.618) in this dead-matter dataset — consistent with none of these being self-organizing living engines. (Earth drifts nearest time-φ, the most dynamically resurfaced body — noted, not claimed.)

## Verdict on the bias question
Not projection. Real, independently-sourced surface features populate the *opposite* pole and the ridge, so the full 0–2 scale is filled by real data — but by three different populations, not rocks alone. The precise closeness to each pole is width-dependent (band shown, W=2→4); the *sign* (soil past the ridge, bodies against the far pole, ripple at the ridge) is width-independent and earned.

**Pole naming is yours to assign** (space/time are swappable in the scale): I've labelled the ends by role — dissipation vs lock — and left the space/time mapping open.

## Fences
Order-of-magnitude timescales; heterogeneous processes (pedogenesis ≠ collisional disruption ≠ denudation); survival capped at the 4.6 Gyr system age; width W is a free convention. Suggestive structure, honestly sourced — not a precision measurement.
