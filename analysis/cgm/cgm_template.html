Blood Glucose — a framework-faithful ARA reading

Healthy (Big Ideas Lab, n=16) vs T1D (D1NAMO, n=9). Per-subject stats, matched detection, φ tested with discipline, the homeostatic-ridge read as the actual map. healthy vs T1D. Replaces an earlier φ test that lacked the proper scaffold (analyze_cgm_phi.py).

Verdict — what held, what broke

The earlier φ test lacked the discipline and was rebuilt properly: declared handover, per-subject, competing constants, a pairing-null, and the between-group prediction. φ is a legitimate framework element (the time-handover) — here it simply isn't the value, which the rule allows.

1 · φ tested properly — it does NOT fall out here φ null

The rise→fall handover ratio (T_fall/T_rise) is a legitimate place φ *should* be expected, so we declared it and tested it with competing constants and a null. Healthy median sits at ~1.4, its 95% CI excludes φ=1.618, and the nearest round constant is 1.5, not φ. The pairing is real (nearer φ than random re-pairing, p=0.015) but its value isn't φ.

2 · The handover ratio does NOT separate healthy from T1D prediction null

The framework's prediction was that T1D's broken insulin handover would degrade the rise→fall ratio. It doesn't: healthy and T1D per-subject medians overlap (Mann-Whitney p≈0.87, Cliff's δ≈−0.05). The handover *timing* is not where diabetes shows up.

3 · The homeostatic ridge — where the diagnosis actually lives ridge separates ±1.0

"Hidden behind apparent stillness": healthy glucose is a tight 1.0-ridge (held in range by opposing flows); T1D is the ridge failing. Variability (CV) and time-in-range separate the groups perfectly (Cliff's δ = ±1.0, p=0.0002). Throughput (normalised flux) does not — so it's containment, not flux rate.

4 · The ridge itself — a healthy record held in range

A healthy subject's glucose (mg/dL) sitting inside the 70–180 target band: the level looks "still," held there by balanced appearance/disposal. In T1D this containment fails (the CV/TIR panels above).

5 · Mapping the process — rungs & couplings

Octave-rung decomposition (normalised — shape, not amplitude). The meal rung (4h) is intact; in T1D the fast rungs (1–2h) are depleted and the variance collapses into slow 8h drift. The coupling matrices show the fast rung losing its cross-scale links in T1D — the ridge-holding coupling wave collapsing.
Bore-in: broken a rung down (fast ~1h feedback — lost power AND cross-scale coupling); energy dumped a rung up (slow 8h); meal rung intact between. CGM 5-min floor limits the 0.5h rung; coupling reverse-inferred from glucose alone (no insulin channel).

6 · Ridge failure decomposed — same event, weaker holding

Same correction frequency and same peak-slope; bigger, longer, faster-mean swings. The containment fails, the event doesn't change.

7 · Separating the overlapping waves (ARA deconvolution)

Meals and boluses are logged together, so their responses overlap. But the two waves sit on different rungs, so a rung-aware deconvolution recovers each impulse-response separately: food drives a faster appearance rise (~1.4h peak), injected insulin a slower disposal fall (~3.4h trough) — ~1.3 octaves apart, disposal SLOWER. That timescale mismatch is what lets glucose escape the band. (Fit R²≈0.02 — food+insulin explain only a slice of glucose; kernel SHAPES are the takeaway, magnitudes soft.)
Honest fences: the ridge metrics that separate the groups (CV, time-in-range) are standard CGM/diabetes measures — ARA's ridge framing recovers the right diagnostic, it doesn't beat clinical practice here. The "extra hidden throughput" idea nulled. n=16 healthy / 7–9 usable T1D, different sensors (matched absolute detection applied). What this run genuinely shows: the earlier undisciplined φ fit, corrected, comes back null on this ratio (a valid "φ doesn't appear here"), and the framework-faithful ridge map points at the correct measurement. φ stays a first-class framework element — here it just isn't the value, exactly the kind of "where φ should not appear" the rule asks us to allow (externally-clocked meal response, physiology sets ~1.4).
