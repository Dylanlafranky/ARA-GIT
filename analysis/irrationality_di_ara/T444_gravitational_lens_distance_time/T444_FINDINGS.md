# T444 findings — real gravitational-lens distance/time decomposition

## Result in one sentence

The frozen conventional lens geometry recovered meaningful real delay ordering but not exact timing: the path and main Connection parent provide a landmark, while the remaining arrival relation still depends on the parent's mass distribution and unmeasured/unused overlapping couplings.

## Exact cut retained

- **Who:** one repeated quasar signal, observed as image children A and B, with foreground deflector G as the external Connection parent.
- **What:** published A–B arrival delay decomposed into a geometric travelled-path term, gravitational-potential term, and unresolved residual.
- **When:** completed published handovers; this is retrospective reconstruction, not live prediction.
- **Where:** Gaia GraL X tables A1 and B1; seven systems survive the frozen exact A/B/G, redshift, delay, and >=150-degree opening requirements.
- **Why:** test whether arrival time is better read as travelled distance inside a Connection relation than as displacement or isolated identity state.
- **How:** two frozen axisymmetric landmarks, point mass and SIS, with flat LCDM conversion H0=70 km/s/Mpc and Omega_m=0.3; no observed delay was used to fit either model.

## Geometry result

The model family changes where the same measured delay relation is assigned:

- **Point mass:** geometric and potential terms are both non-zero and sit near a balanced ARA contribution share around (1,1).
- **SIS:** the ideal differential geometric terms cancel, placing the delay at the Connection-heavy pole (0,2).

This is not a change in the identity of the light. It is a change in the assumed distribution of the external parent. It therefore supports the methodological refinement that an identity's path cannot be predicted from its own state alone: the anti-phase/Connection field must be specified.

## Important negative result

Departure from perfect 180-degree sky-plane alignment did **not** cleanly explain the remaining factor error:

- point mass: rho=0.036, p=0.939;
- SIS: rho=0.321, p=0.482.

The visible opening angle is therefore not an adequate proxy for the omitted overlapping identities. The residual still bundles mass-profile error, ellipticity, external shear, line-of-sight convergence, cosmology, astrometric uncertainty, and time-delay systematics. It cannot be called a newly detected force or hidden identity.
