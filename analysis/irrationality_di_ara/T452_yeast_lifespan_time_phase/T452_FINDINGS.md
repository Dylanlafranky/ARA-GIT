# T452 — yeast lifespan Phase A–B reconstruction

## Result in one sentence

A reproductive-maturity phase constructed only from generation count and a clock-time phase constructed only from recorded hours recover the same bowed interior path, late local-rate ridge crossing, and ordered slowdown in an untouched same-platform cohort and a separate 119-cell platform cohort.

## The central numbers

- Cells: `225` (`94` development, `12` untouched experiment-9 holdout, `119` external cohort).
- Development versus holdout signed time-shadow correlation: `0.9851`.
- Development versus external signed time-shadow correlation: `0.9848`.
- Local division-time child curve correlations: `0.9811` holdout and `0.9902` external.
- Local equal-rate ridge crossing:
  - development: `A=1.4551`;
  - holdout: `A=1.4040`;
  - external: `A=1.4532`.
- Median late-minus-early normalized interval rate:
  - development: `0.6649`;
  - holdout: `0.7130` versus shuffled 95% limit `0.1917`;
  - external: `0.7714` versus shuffled 95% limit `0.0538`.
- Empirical upper-tail shuffle probability: `1/2001 ≈ 0.00050` in each cohort.
- Frozen gates: `6/6` pass.

## What the shape says

The elapsed clock coordinate stays below equal generation progress through most of the observed lifespan. In the counter-traversing view this is the same as `maturity + remaining time` sitting above the pure total `2`. The signed shadow reaches its deepest point around `A=1.30–1.55`, while the local interval rate crosses its own ridge near `A=1.4–1.46` and then accelerates sharply. The accumulated shadow minimum and local-rate crossing are related landmarks but are not the same cut.

Cell area grows and accelerates late in both platforms. Rpl13A-GFP concentration generally declines, while the derived total abundance rises late as size growth outweighs concentration dilution. These lower children align with the clock slowdown but do not identify Time by themselves.

## Established science crosswalk

The source paper already reports late-life lengthening of division times, increasing cell size, age-dependent Rpl13A behaviour, and a cell-specific Senescence Entry Point identified by an elbow in division duration. T452 did not use the author's SEP labels. It recovered a population handover in the same biological territory by a separately declared ARA maturity/time construction.

## What this does not prove

This is a reconstruction of completed observed lifespans. Both endpoints close by definition, and each cell's full span is needed to normalize the axes. It does not yet predict the death of a living cell, measure the omitted terminal interval, establish that the shadow is Time itself, or prove a universal lifespan law.

## Next test

Use only partial life histories to predict the onset of sustained `local rate > 1` and remaining divisions on untouched cells, with the `A≈1.455` development crossing and full population curves frozen in advance. A later cross-species test must use an independently recorded birth-to-death source and declare the new scale and orientation before results are opened.
