## Frozen translation

This test asks a narrower question than whether quantum mechanics is "pure information" or contains a hidden
Phase B:

> When the correct quantum child is already named, does the fixed ARA time-stream instrument recover its omitted
> source/sink waveform better than a conventional causal state-space tracker using the same observations?

The compared methods receive:

- the same declared child identity;
- the same noisy stored quantity \(q_{\rm obs}(t)\);
- the same noisy declared transfer \(g_{\rm obs}(t)\);
- the same sample times;
- the same observed-only calibration prefix.

Neither method receives the native hidden waveform during target operation. The state-space comparator may estimate
observation-noise scales from the calibration prefix, but may not use clean target values, the native sink law or
target scores.

## Interpretation boundary

Three conclusions must remain separate:

1. **Absolute tracking quality:** whether fixed ARA lineage remains an adequate quantum waveform tracker.
2. **Comparative method result:** whether ARA is better than, worse than, or practically similar to the conventional
   state-space tracker.
3. **Ontology:** neither outcome demonstrates that quantum is pure information, establishes a hidden Phase B, or
   identifies a human perception channel.

If the state-space tracker matches or beats ARA, O2-A2 can still be a good tracking result, but its performance is
not an ARA-specific advantage. If ARA wins by frozen practical margins, that motivates independent replication on
new generators and real calibrated observations.
