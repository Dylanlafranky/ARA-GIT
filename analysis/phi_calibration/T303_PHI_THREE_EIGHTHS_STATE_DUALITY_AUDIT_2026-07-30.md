## Answer first

The proposed distinction is useful:

\[
\underbrace{\frac38}_{\substack{\text{local connection}\\
\text{or closing step}}}
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{\phi^{-2}}_{\substack{\text{ordered motion}\\
\text{or non-closing carrier}}}.
\]

It explains the split that motivated the audit. In T302 phyllotaxis, `3/8`
was the best fixed **one-placement** constant while exact \(\phi^{-2}\) was the
best fixed **cumulative-position** constant. That is direct evidence that a
local step and its longer ordered carrier should not automatically be assigned
the same constant.

The repository does not yet support the stronger claim that every connected
state is exactly `3/8` and every moving parent is exactly Phi:

- the canonical ARA⁹ lattice contains nine relation cells and three retained
  Bell directions, giving `3/9 = 1/3`, not `3/8`;
- no existing ARA⁹ motion test predeclared a commensurate Phi-versus-`3/8`
  phase-advance coordinate;
- Q43 directly tested a motion/handover coordinate and did not find exact Phi
  privileged;
- several connected systems prefer rational closure or recurrence, but their
  winners are quarters, thirds or zero recurrence rather than a universal
  `3/8`.

The defensible reduced claim is:

> ARA measurements should distinguish a locally closing structural step from
> the ordered motion of its larger carrier. Connected cuts often rationalize
> or recur; moving non-closing carriers can favour irrational advances. T302
> supplies one clean `3/8`/Phi example, not a universal constant law.

## 1. ARA⁹ quantum audit

### 1.1 Connection-side lattice

Q24 defines the connected ARA⁹ object as a \(3\times3\) relation tensor:

\[
C=T-\mathbf a\mathbf b^\mathsf T.
\]

The calibrated ladder is:

- Bell records: three retained relation directions;
- classical mixtures: one retained direction;
- uniform mixture: zero retained directions.

The nine cells are canonical. Therefore the immediately available occupancy
fraction is

\[
\frac{\text{three retained directions}}{\text{nine relation cells}}
=\frac39=\frac13.
\]

Deleting the central cell after seeing the result would manufacture an
eight-cell denominator and is not allowed. The many `Phi-plus` and
`Phi-minus` strings in these files are Bell-state names \(\Phi^\pm\), not
golden-ratio measurements.

**Finding:** the ARA⁹ lattice strongly supports a structured connected object,
but it does not contain a measured `3/8` coordinate.

**Target correction from Dylan:** the intended candidate does not live in
the lattice's cell count. The complete nine-cell lattice is the local
identity; ARA⁹ is to be followed in its **meta movement** from one complete
lattice state to the next. Thus `3/9 = 1/3` blocks a wrong occupancy
derivation but neither tests nor rejects the intended moving-lattice claim.

## 3. Golden Stars correction

T303 rediscovered a sign inconsistency in the old headline file, then found
that it had already been formally corrected and independently rerun on
2 July 2026 in `GOLDEN_STARS_CORRECTION.md`. The correction, not the old
31 May wording, is the canonical result.

The historical report defined:

- \(d=|P_x/P_{1O}-1/\phi|\), so smaller \(d\) means closer to exact Phi;
- lower `R21` means leaner.

It then reported

\[
\operatorname{corr}(d,R21)=-0.347
\]

and described this as “closer to Phi = leaner.” That interpretation is
mathematically reversed. A negative correlation says that larger distance is
associated with lower `R21`; within this sample, moving **away** from exact
Phi was associated with the leaner value.

The 2 July re-run strengthened the class comparison:

- club `n=949`, control `n=17,305`;
- mean `R21` `0.1138` versus `0.1250`, an `8.9%` gap;
- Mann–Whitney `p=5.7e-8`;
- period/amplitude-matched mean difference `−0.0077`, Wilcoxon
  `p=8.8e-43`, `n=946`;
- the within-club inverse gradient survived controls: partial
  `r=−0.203`, `p=2.7e-10`.

Those values were replicated on the author's hardware. This supports “the
RR0.61 class is leaner on this measure,” not “closeness to exact Phi causes
leanness.” The remaining documented confound is whether the additional
non-radial mode affects the primary-mode `R21` fit; prewhitening that secondary
mode is the decisive follow-up.

The exact-Phi “backbone” claim is therefore rejected by the corrected
replication.

## 4. What the `3/8 + 3/8 + 3/8 = 9/8` arithmetic establishes

\[
3\left(\frac38\right)=\frac98=1+\frac18.
\]

This is exact arithmetic and supplies a clean candidate generator: three
local `3/8` steps complete one turn and advance one eighth into the next.
It does not by itself show that a measured physical system uses that
generator. Under the T303 evidence rules it is a **forced mathematical
consequence**, not an empirical result.

The suitable test is to freeze a three-step residual before opening an
independent ordered system and compare:

- `3/8` residual: `1/8 = 0.125`;
- exact-Phi residual:
  \(3\phi^{-2}\bmod1=0.145898\ldots\);
- thirds, fifths, `2/5`, `sqrt(2)-1`, and fitted-development controls.

## 5. Decisive ARA⁹ follow-up

The current simulator is exactly diagonal, so it can only reveal `0°/180°`
reorientations. The next dataset must contain repeated, time-resolved,
off-diagonal connected \(3\times3\) relations in one stable frame. Each
complete \(C(t)\), not each individual cell, is one point on the
meta-trajectory.

Before opening the evaluation half, freeze two measurements from the same
cycles:

1. **Connection cut:** the one-step local child allocation or closure advance.
   Score `3/8`, Phi and fixed rational/irrational rivals.
2. **Carrier cut:** normalize away total lattice magnitude,
   \(\widehat C(t)=C(t)/\lVert C(t)\rVert_F\), then measure the cumulative
   orientation/path of the entire connected tensor across several child
   steps. Score the same constants without changing normalization.

Required result:

- `3/8` uniquely wins the local connected cut;
- Phi uniquely wins the cumulative moving-carrier cut;
- both repeat across a held-out archive and beat time-order and disconnected
  controls.

That would test exactly the distinction proposed here. The current ARA⁹
visuals are a reason to run it, not already the result.
