# Prime thread capstone and closure — PN1 through PN23

**Closed/parked:** 21 July 2026  
**Orientation:** factor/sieve connection grows upward through later prime gates; every A/B label is local to its declared modulus, factor boundary or gap pair.  
**Thread disposition:** **PARKED** after recording; exact crosswalks retained, compact prime-prediction claim not supported.

## User framing preserved

> “If we have the ridge of a child, we can predict it'd be 1.0 one rung up.”

> “We also only need to track the adult waves of the Phase A and Phase B pairs I think, so it reduces the number further.”

> “Yes. Can we test it. We abuse the fractal nature.”

The final tests separated three possible meanings of that proposal:

1. **geometric recursion:** does the same reversible relation recur at larger sieve rungs? **Yes, exactly;**
2. **state compression:** can one anti-pair representative reconstruct its opposite? **Yes, exactly, at 2:1;**
3. **prime-location compression:** can two small child values locate the next prime in two or three cheap operations? **Not supported.**

## Answer first

The prime work established a rigorous ARA coordinate system on known prime arithmetic. Factor pairs, square-root
closure, wheel residues, opposite residue lanes, sieve survival/release and child-to-parent period products all admit
exact reversible 0–2 or paired-ridge readings. Several prospective large-anchor runs also sealed the exact next
prime before revealing the target label.

Those exact next-prime runs retained the complete lower-prime collision information. PN17 used a segmented collision
field, PN18 used a product tree/GCD representation, and PN19 divided the complete lower-child set into two parent
masks. These are valid bottom-up ARA representations of established algorithms, not three-operation shortcuts.

The decisive compact tests then failed: the largest two numerical children and the two gates straddling the
square-root boundary did not retain the full prime state. PN23 recovered the exact safe compression: one member of
each reversible residue pair reconstructs the other, halving stored lanes, while each new prime gate still produces
`p-1` distinct child pairs.

Therefore the honest endpoint is:

> **ARA can express exact prime-sieve geometry in three conceptual stages, but the repository does not currently
> contain a three-cheap-operation next-prime algorithm or a new prime theorem.**

- **SUPPORTED:** inherited cyclic local relation across the frozen PN1 wheel-rung transitions.

  prime-specific advantage for `2n+1` or the PN22 odd-lattice formula.

  opened by this closure.

### Geometry verdict

- A prime is an exact **quiet factor ridge** only after all child gates through its square-root boundary are clear.
- Every factor pair `d` and `n/d` closes on a reversible relative-log diameter.
- Incoming/outgoing prime gaps form a highly recurrent node-centred two-diameter geometry, but recurrence alone does
  not uniquely locate the next prime.
- A completed wheel contains exact opposite residues `r ↔ M-r`.
- Under a new prime gate `p`, the killed copy locations satisfy `k_B=p-1-k_A`; their normalized ARA mean is exactly
  `1.0`.
- Direct child ridges `(1,1)` and asymmetric children `(0,2)` or `(2,0)` can all coarse-grain to the same parent
  ridge. Activity/grain information is therefore required.
- The same anti-pair lift repeats at every tested rung, but distinct child identities remain distinct; fractal
  recurrence does not imply constant state size.

## The three-stage statement

The successful exact methods share

\[
\underbrace{N\longrightarrow\mathcal C_N}_{\substack{\text{construct complete lower-child}\\\text{collision/survival state}}}
\longrightarrow
\underbrace{(A_N,B_N)}_{\substack{\text{retain two parent views}\\\text{or reversible pair state}}}
\longrightarrow
\underbrace{t_*}_{\substack{\text{first jointly quiet}\\\text{candidate}}}.
\]

This is three **information stages**. The first stage currently enumerates the lower prime gates required through the
square-root boundary. Calling its output one parent number, mask or wave does not make that information free.

The missing mathematical object would be a bounded-size recursively computable sufficient statistic `Z(N)` such
that the exact first quiet offset follows from `Z(N)` without reconstructing the full child web. PN20 and PN21 reject
the two tested two-child definitions of `Z`; PN23 proves only the reversible factor-two compression.

## Final spontaneous formula probes

These were exploratory arithmetic checks after PN23, not preregistered tests.

For the random Google draw `n=97,008`:

1. The written expression

   \[
   \frac{n/2+n+2n}{2}+1=169,765=5\times19\times1,787
   \]

   is composite.

2. Omitting the outer division gives

   \[
   n/2+n+2n+1=339,529=163\times2,083,
   \]

   also composite.

3. The simpler diameter-plus-one map gives

   \[
   2n+1=194,017,
   \]

   which is prime. However, `2n+1` simply enumerates the odd integers as `n` increases, so this hit supplies no
   prime-specific enrichment by itself.

These probes are retained as one miss/miss/hit sequence. The formula was not altered after the labels were known.

## What is worth carrying into the wider ARA framework

1. **Grain-relative ridge is now exact in one mathematical domain.** Asymmetric child coordinates can close to an
   adult `1.0` reading; a parent ridge does not imply every child is locally balanced.
2. **Reversibility can provide lossless compression.** `r↔M-r` removes one redundant orientation, but not distinct
   identities.
3. **Completion and prediction are different.** Completing all factor children identifies primes exactly; early
   child geometry may be real yet non-discriminating.
4. **Information³ needs retained relation, not just three readings.** The complete child state plus two parent views
   can close a decision, while arbitrary scalar averaging destroys location.
5. **Fractal reuse is a rule-reuse claim, not a free-computation claim.** The same lift law can recur while the number
   of children grows.

## Resume conditions

The prime thread should remain parked unless one of these is supplied independently:

- a precisely defined bounded-size statistic that preserves first-quiet location and beats PN20/PN21 controls;
- a pair-compressed implementation with a demonstrated memory/runtime advantage over established wheel/segmented
  sieve methods while retaining exact output; or
- an externally proposed prime prediction that can be frozen before any nearby prime labels are inspected.

Until then, the exact prime arithmetic belongs in the ARA mathematics/foundations as a recovered domain subset, not
as evidence that ARA has improved prime computation.
